\section{Discussion, Limitations, and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Across over $140,000$ passes on a controlled multi-step benchmark, we showed that: (1) uncalibrated continuous slots induce $-10.33$ to $-20.00$pp multi-step interference under intact markers, $100\%$ recovered by \texttt{Zero-Slot} (Proposition~\ref{prop:margin}); (2) decoding-time vision masking (\texttt{E5}) routes multi-step answers via prefill caches $\KV_{Q,M}$ rather than 28-layer slot caches $\KV_H$ (\texttt{E6-C}, Proposition~\ref{prop:bypass}); and (3) under a LIVR/CVRR-style strict image-free interface (Proposition~\ref{prop:isolation}), step-aligned 2-slot sub-windows (\texttt{E6-A}) achieve $72.67\%$--$83.33\%$ in-domain chain accuracy ($+63.67$ to $+69.00$pp over shared prototypes \texttt{E6-B}, $80.49$--$81.25$pp zero-drop on $\Vorig$) and $+2.75$ to $+3.58$ matched-window conditional log-odds shifts, while discrete flips on recolored unseen \texttt{yellow/brown} targets remain at $0.00\%$ ($K=2$) and $3.12\%$ ($K=4$; $5$ eligible records / $4$ clusters).

\textbf{Limitations.} Three boundaries frame our results:
(i) \emph{Domain vs.\ hop count}: single-hop neutrality (\texttt{Pilot}) was measured on public benchmarks whereas multi-step interference (\texttt{E3-C}) was measured on composited/recolored COCO scenes, motivating a same-scene $K=1$ 8-arm control;
(ii) \emph{Parallel attribute encoding vs.\ sequential reasoning}: because Phase A emits $H_{\mathrm{diff}}$ in a single parallel pass with step-wise ROI MSE and 8-color CE supervision, \texttt{E6-A} may act as a parallel per-target attribute encoder rather than performing multi-hop latent computation, and $H$'s necessity in Phase B is enforced by removing $\VT$ rather than surpassing adapted \texttt{NoLatent} ($70.00\%$) or \texttt{Text-CoT} ($68.33\%$--$71.33\%$); and
(iii) \emph{Prior regularization}: broad counterfactual flipping requires balanced color supervision or prior regularization against the base VLM.
